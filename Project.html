First Attempts

I made two false starts. Firstly, I started off by using MovieLens, but then realised this dataset was used in Unit 8 and decided I should use something else.

I searched for databases that interested me - I'm an avid reader and I love the theatre. I couldn't find any suitable theatrical databases, they were all very one-dimensional. I found a reasonably good dataset on books called "Book Crossing".

After working with the Book Crossing dataset for a little while, I found it just didn't have the breadth of information that MovieLens did. There was much less information about the users, for example.

At this point, I decided to return to MovieLens, but to use the 1M dataset, rather than the 100k dataset that was used in Unit 8. This meant I could use some of the Unit 8 code and just adapt it for the slightly different data. I also changed every reference to "Movie" into "Film", because "Movie" is an Americanism that I find really irritating!

Reflections

I found this extremely difficult.

I had to learn so much in a very short space of time and I found simple things like how to update my Github page and how to record the presentation challenging - these were not things I'd done before.

I really enjoyed delving into the information. With my Book Crossing and with my Movie Lens data, I looked up films and books that I liked to see what other people thought of them. This was why I chose a recommender system rather than Computer Vision, as I could bring my own interests and knowledge in to play.

I considered what I would do if I worked for a smaller film company. Would I develop a recommender system that looked only at the quality of ratings, and ignored the volume, so that films like mine would feature more prominently in the recommendations? Would that be ethical?

This project was a struggle for me. I enjoyed many aspects of it, but it was not without its frustrations at my own lack of knowledge. I will probably work through the process again on the same data to try and improve my skills in Python.
